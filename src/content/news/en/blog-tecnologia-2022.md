### The rapid digital acceleration we are experiencing has made technology the cornerstone of global leadership
### Technology trends to keep in mind
In recent months there has been a radical change in the behavior of consumers, users and the world in general.
In these years in particular, as a side effect of the global pandemic, the need has arisen to adopt new technologies and trends that will permanently change the way we do business and the way we live. We must adapt to the new reality: only the companies that have made technology the foundation of their processes have become successful, and they are the ones most likely to survive in the market. It is now clear that there is no leadership without technological leadership.
> The rapid digital acceleration we are experiencing has made technology the cornerstone of global leadership.
In this report we look at the technologies that are not just here to stay, but here to define the coming years.
If the premise for 2020 was survival, in 2021 it is getting back on the path to growth by recovering the businesses that stalled during the pandemic. Only a solid digital strategy will make companies more flexible and competitive, able to adapt to changes in their environment and contribute to business continuity, process efficiency and, above all, security.
For 2022, the indicators tell us that the pandemic will keep affecting our lives in many ways, which means companies will need to adopt an optimal digitalization strategy.
Next year's most important trends are likely to focus on the convergence of technology trends, as tools emerge that make it possible to combine them.
Following their recent studies, analysts point out that in 2022 companies will need to combine their technology deployment with economic growth and market opportunities, leading to the adoption of trends that solve the main challenges they will face.
### Which trends should be on our radar?
### Artificial intelligence
Artificial intelligence (AI) is becoming ever more pervasive, not only in smartphones and voice assistants, but also as the driver of much of the robotic process automation that has lightened workloads in administration, logistics, accounting and human resources departments. We see AI-powered solutions in every industry, and this will grow even further next year.
In 2022 we will also see the progress of what Gartner calls **generative AI**, which it describes as machine learning methods that learn about content or objects from their data and use it to generate brand-new, original, realistic artifacts. Tasks such as writing software code, facilitating drug development and targeted marketing could proliferate thanks to generative AI.
However, the firm also warns that AI can be used for scams, fraud, forgeries and disinformation. Even so, it is expected to account for 10% of all data produced by 2025, compared with less than 1% today.
### Cloud platforms
The new and urgent needs that emerged over the last two years have accelerated cloud adoption around the world. Cloud-native platforms will also play an important role throughout 2022 in delivering capabilities anywhere.
Gartner predicts that cloud-native platforms will be the foundation for more than 95% of new digital initiatives by 2025, compared with less than 40% at the end of 2021.
### Autonomous systems
Autonomous systems will also be among next year's leading technology trends and will become common in products such as robots, drones and manufacturing machines. Unlike automated systems, autonomous systems can dynamically modify their own algorithms without an external software update, allowing them to adapt quickly to new conditions.
Gartner highlights autonomous systems as a longer-term trend and expects them to mature over the next five years.
### Automation and hyperautomation
In 2022, automation will remain in the spotlight. Technologies such as robotic process automation, natural language processing and intelligent document processing will keep reducing dependence on human labor.
But automation will move towards hyperautomation: everything that can be optimized will be optimized, offering business leaders major benefits when it comes to quickly identifying, examining and automating as many processes as possible.
### Democratization: everything as a service and the no-code revolution
Another big technology trend for 2022 will be the democratization of data and technology. In recent years a whole industry has emerged with the aim of putting the skills and tools needed for innovation in the hands of as much of society as possible, regardless of their experience or knowledge.
Innovation has been held back in some areas by the skills crisis which, although it may be seen as a problem, has been the driving force behind the explosion of low-code solutions. In 2022 we will keep seeing companies deploy AI and IoT infrastructures without owning a single server or proprietary cognitive code.
No-code interfaces will become more popular as a lack of programming skills, or of an understanding of statistics and data structures, stops being a barrier to making a world-changing idea a reality.
### The distributed enterprise
Major technology changes will respond to ongoing adjustments in operating models, as most companies keep fine-tuning their hybrid working models.
According to Gartner, by 2023 three quarters of the organizations that exploit the benefits of a distributed business model, which lets companies operate while their workers are geographically dispersed, will grow their revenue 25% faster than their peers.
> The distributed enterprise will replace traditional organizations.
With millions of people around the world still working remotely, Gartner points out that the distributed enterprise will probably replace the office-centered organizations of recent years.
### Data silos
As the number of data and application silos keeps growing, there will be a need for structures that enable flexible, resilient data integration across platforms and business users.
### Predictive analytics
Together, data analytics, data lake consolidation and predictive analytics will be major trends across the IT industry. Even IoT and cybersecurity are strongly driven by advanced predictive analytics techniques.
Predictive analytics tools will keep changing the rules of the game next year in almost every industry and business domain.
### Sensors and connected machines
Sensors and connected machines are at the peak of an ongoing revolution, taking us towards a world where everything is measured. Combining sensor data with far-reaching analytics at the edge and in the cloud will act as a springboard for innovation and for predicting the future.
### Composable applications
Companies that adopt a composable approach can outpace their competitors by 80% in the speed of new feature implementation. This is an extension of the concept of the composable organization which, according to Gartner, can help companies respond to the change they are immersed in.
### Edge cloud
Sovereignty needs and edge computing are the new challenges for technology leaders. Cloud adoption has transformed IT organizations, operating models and governance.
Computing capabilities need to move closer to users and resources for real-time processing. Edge computing and distributed clouds will counteract the extreme centralization of the cloud.
### Cybersecurity mesh
There should be a distributed approach to network security, with individual perimeters around each access point instead of a traditional, all-encompassing top-down approach.
The cybersecurity mesh shifts the focus from protecting a traditional IT perimeter to a more modular approach that centralizes policy orchestration but distributes cybersecurity enforcement.
Gartner predicts that by 2024, companies that adopt a cybersecurity mesh will reduce the financial impact of security incidents by 90%.
### Total experience (TX)
Total experience remains among next year's big technology trends, as the value of the combined experiences of customers, employees and users will define the potential to improve business results.
However, companies need to break down each of these silos and understand the benefits of improving the experience of all stakeholders.
### Digitalization, data and virtualization
In 2022 we will become increasingly familiar with the concept of the “metaverse”: persistent digital worlds that exist in parallel with the physical world we live in. As the pace of digitalization increases, these metaverses will model and simulate the real world with ever greater accuracy, giving us more immersive, compelling and ultimately valuable experiences in the digital realm.
While many of us have experienced somewhat immersive virtual realities through headsets, a range of new devices coming to market soon will greatly improve the experience by offering haptic feedback and even smells.
### Sustainable technology trends
During the most critical months of the pandemic, non-renewable energy use fell worldwide as industries shut down and people stayed at home, leading to an overall 8% reduction in CO2 emissions. This has raised expectations of increasing investment in renewable energy generation in the coming years.
The International Energy Agency (IEA) estimates that 40% more renewable energy was generated and used in 2020 than in the previous year, and forecasts that this growth will continue throughout 2022.
### Replacing legacy systems
The move towards greater digitalization will keep leading many organizations to replace their obsolete legacy systems, which also increase the risk of an attack or breach.
Security vulnerabilities are an unintended, natural result of deploying a patchwork of outdated, inefficient solutions. Assessing and modernizing the technology and systems stack will be the goal of more and more organizations that want to stay competitive and secure.
Not all of these trends will affect every organization equally: as you know, every company is a world of its own, and it will be up to IT leaders to choose which are the highest priority or have the greatest impact for their organizations, since they are the ones who must understand their strategic and short-term business goals.
